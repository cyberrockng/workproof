These answers describe the current hackathon deployment and the intended production model.
What is WorkProof?

A Coston2 escrow protocol where smart-contract work is paid only after a Flare Confidential Compute verifier signs a matching PASS verdict.

What does the TEE verify?

It verifies committed hidden test vectors against the submitted artifact, rechecks chain state, derives vector selection from Flare randomness, and signs VerdictV1.

What happens on FAIL?

The contractor is not paid. The job can move to resubmission if still within the allowed window, or refund behavior if too late.

Can the client steal funds?

The client cannot withdraw while the job is live and validly progressing. Refund requires missed acceptance/submission/verification windows or expiry behavior defined by the contract.

Can the contractor be paid without passing?

No. The escrow pays only after it verifies a PASS verdict signed by the pinned TEE with all required bindings.

Is this mainnet-ready?

No. The current project is hackathon/testnet evidence. Production requires hardware attestation, formal audit, RPC quorum, monitoring, and operational controls.

Is this real hardware attestation?

No. The current Coston2 deployment uses simulated attestation (`magic_pass`, `TEST_PLATFORM`) as allowed for the hackathon demo.

What is simulated attestation?

It is a development mode where the FCC stack exercises the registration and verification flow without proving that the workload ran inside real GCP AMD SEV Confidential Space hardware.
